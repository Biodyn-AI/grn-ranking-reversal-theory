\title{Ranking Reversal Theory Under Candidate-Set Shift\\in GRN Benchmarking}

\author{Anonymous Authors}
\affiliation{%

\begin{abstract}
When method~$A$ scores higher than method~$B$ on a gene regulatory
network (GRN) benchmark, does the rank still hold if we change the
evaluation protocol --- candidate edges or reference network? The answer
hinges on the \emph{margin} between the methods' scores, and we give a
closed-form rule for when the rank is safe. Across four protocol axes
on six methods and three single-cell tissues we find: candidate-set
changes flip $16\%$ of method pairs (cluster-bootstrap 95\%~CI
$7$--$27\%$); cross-tissue, $19\%$ ($9$--$31\%$); reference-network,
$32\%$ ($20$--$43\%$); gene-symbol mapping, none of $165$. The popular
``base-rate~$\times$~discrimination'' decomposition has two pieces the
algebra forces (no data information); the data-carrying piece is the
relative \emph{magnitude} of the two terms, which we find large and
significant (rank-biserial $r = 0.67$, cluster-permutation
$p < 2\!\times\!10^{-4}$). A single leaderboard number is not biological
evidence on its own: reports should include protocol stability, margin
regime, and metric.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

GRN benchmark leaderboards are read as evidence for biological
plausibility~\cite{cui2024scgpt,theodoris2023}, but behind every score is
a chain of protocol choices --- candidate edges, reference network,
gene-symbol mapping, tissue --- rarely reported or
controlled~\cite{pratapa2020,aibar2017,huynhthu2010,anon2025evalbias}. The
problem is familiar in ML: BLEU re-ranks under different
tokenizers~\cite{post2018}; ``benchmark lottery'' effects swap
leaderboards across dataset
choices~\cite{dehghani2021,demsar2006,critchlow1985}. When two methods'
ranks flip with the protocol, the biological consequence flips too: which
regulator gets validated, which model is called credible. We need a way
to say when a rank is robust.

\emph{Contributions.} \emph{(1)}~A closed-form ``safety margin'' per
method pair (\S\ref{sec:theory}). \emph{(2)}~A reading of the
base-rate-vs.-discrimination decomposition separating sign claims (forced
by algebra) from magnitude claims (an actual data fact)
(\S\ref{sec:magnitude}). \emph{(3)}~An audit across four protocol axes
with cluster-bootstrap intervals, three nulls, a margin sweep, a learned
classifier (honest negative), and a metric-robustness
check~(\S\ref{sec:results}).

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let $\mathcal{M}_m(S,\pi,R)$ be a benchmark score (such
as AUPR) for method $m$ under candidate set $S$, gene-symbol mapping
$\pi$, and reference network $R$. The \emph{margin} between methods $A$ and
$B$ is the score gap $\Delta = \mathcal{M}_A - \mathcal{M}_B$, and a
\emph{ranking reversal} between two protocol settings is the event
$\Delta_1 \Delta_2 < 0$ --- the gap flips sign. Writing
$\Delta = b \cdot g$ with $b$ the positive-class base rate and $g := \Delta/b$
the base-rate-normalized gap, the standard ``base-rate $\times$
discrimination'' decomposition follows directly from the product rule:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:decomp}
\Delta_2 - \Delta_1 = \underbrace{(b_2 - b_1)\,g_1}_{\text{base-rate term}}
                    + \underbrace{b_2\,(g_2 - g_1)}_{\text{calibration term}}.
\end{equation}

The current margin $|\Delta_1|$ is the ``cushion'': for the rank to flip,
the shift must first eat through it and overshoot in the opposite
direction (Prop.~\ref{prop:minshift}).

\begin{proposition}[Minimum-shift bound]
\label{prop:minshift}
If every shift in a family satisfies $|\delta\Delta| \le B$, then no method
pair with margin $|\Delta_1| > B$ can reverse. More tightly, a reversal of
a pair with margin $|\Delta_1|$ requires
\(
|b_2-b_1|\,|g_1| + b_2\,|g_2-g_1| \ge |\Delta_1|.
\)
In particular, if discrimination is shift-stable ($|g_2-g_1| \le
\varepsilon_g$), any reversing base-rate change must satisfy
$|b_2-b_1| \ge (|\Delta_1| - b_2\varepsilon_g)/|g_1|$ (informative when
$b_2\varepsilon_g < |\Delta_1|$).
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Sign counts from Eq.~\ref{eq:decomp} are forced by algebra.}
Under positive base rates, holding $g$ fixed gives $(b_2/b_1)\Delta_1^2>0$
(a base-rate change alone never flips the sign); holding $b$ fixed gives
$(b_1/b_2)\Delta_1\Delta_2 < 0$ on every reversal by definition. So the
``$0/22$ vs.\ $22/22$'' counts reported by earlier
versions~\cite{anon2025evalbias} restate the definition --- the only
data-bearing piece is the magnitude comparison (\S\ref{sec:magnitude}).

\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\paragraph{Data.} Published benchmark medians of AUPR, AUROC, and F1
across three Tabula~Sapiens tissues~\cite{tabulasapiens2022} (kidney, lung,
immune) $\times$ three candidate-set definitions, on six methods:
\textsc{genie3}~\cite{huynhthu2010}, \textsc{grnboost2}, \textsc{pearson},
\textsc{spearman}, \textsc{scgpt\_attention}~\cite{cui2024scgpt},
\textsc{random}. Reference shifts on the immune slice with three
reference networks (\textsc{hpn\_dream},
\textsc{beeline\_gsd}~\cite{pratapa2020}, a
\textsc{dorothea}~\cite{holland2020}/\textsc{trrust}~\cite{han2018} union);
mapping shifts on a probe-prior subset over four symbol-resolution policies
(\textsc{omnipath}~\cite{turei2021} included).

\paragraph{Analyses.}
\emph{(i)}~per-pair reversal indicator $\mathbf{1}\{\Delta_1\Delta_2 < 0\}$;
\emph{(ii)}~\textbf{cluster-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals} treating
each method pair as one cluster~\cite{cameron2015} (Wilson intervals would
shrink the CI artificially by assuming independence);
\emph{(iii)}~a \textbf{cluster-restricted permutation Mann--Whitney test}
on the magnitude-ratio comparison, with 5{,}000 within-cluster
permutations of the reversal label;
\emph{(iv)}~per-cell Benjamini--Hochberg false discovery rate;
\emph{(v)}~a margin-magnitude sweep over a threshold $\tau$;
\emph{(vi)}~three null models: random method-label permutation, Gaussian
noise ($\sigma = 10\%$ of within-cell median margin), within-cell rank
permutation;
\emph{(vii)}~leave-one-tissue-out reversal prediction: quantile heuristic
vs.\ logistic regression on $|\Delta_1|, b_1, b_2{-}b_1, |g_1|$;
\emph{(viii)}~metric robustness across AUPR/AUROC/F1.
All seeds fixed at 42; decomposition closure verified below $10^{-9}$.

\subsection{Candidate-set and tissue axes}
\label{sec:tissue}

The marginal $16\%$ candidate-shift rate is driven by the immune tissue
and by pairs of methods that were already close (\S\ref{sec:margin}). After
per-cell Benjamini--Hochberg correction, \emph{none} of the high-rate
cells highlighted in the workshop precursor (e.g.\ the immune all-pairs to
TF-source-target shift at 6/15) survive $q < 0.05$. What does survive is
the opposite: cells that are significantly \emph{more stable} than chance
(e.g.\ the kidney TF-source to TF-source-target shift at 0/15,
$q = 5 \times 10^{-4}$).

For tissue shifts, the same method pair appears in three candidate-set
cells, so counting them separately inflates the signal. When we
deduplicate to one row per (method-pair, tissue-pair) and ask ``does this
pair flip under at least one candidate set?'', the rate is 0.38 (17/45);
asking ``does it flip under \emph{all} candidate sets?'' gives 0.02
(1/45). The honest single-number summary is the ``flips at least once''
rate, 0.38.

\subsection{Magnitude decomposition}
\label{sec:magnitude}

In all 22 candidate-shift reversal rows, the discrimination (``calibration'')
term is larger in magnitude than the base-rate term ($22/22$ vs.\ $56/113$
in non-reversal rows). The mean ratio
$|\text{cal}|/|\text{base-rate}|$ is $1.54$ in reversals versus $0.83$ in
non-reversals.

A naive Mann--Whitney test would give $p = 3.6 \times 10^{-7}$, but the
135 rows are not independent --- they come from only 15 unique method-pair
clusters (and only 7 of those clusters contain any reversal). To respect
that dependence we ran a \emph{cluster-restricted} permutation Mann--Whitney
test that shuffles the reversal labels only \emph{within} each
method-pair cluster (5{,}000 permutations). The observed test statistic
$U = 2075$ sits above the null $97.5\%$ quantile ($[1426, 1907]$), with
rank-biserial effect size $r = 0.67$ (large) and permutation
$p < 2 \times 10^{-4}$ (no null draw exceeded the observed $U$). This
cluster-aware $p$ is the one we report; the iid $p$ above is too
optimistic.

\subsection{Margin regime, nulls, screening, metric}
\label{sec:margin}

\paragraph{Margin sweep.} Discarding method pairs whose initial gap is
below the 75th percentile, candidate-shift reversals vanish ($0/34$);
reference-shift reversals do not (rate stays in $[0.22, 0.38]$). So
candidate-shift instability is a \emph{near-tie phenomenon}, while
reference-shift instability survives clear gaps.

\paragraph{Three nulls.} The observed $0.16$ sits well below all three:
random method-label permutation gives null mean $0.50$ ($[0.37, 0.61]$);
within-cell rank permutation, the same; Gaussian noise at $\sigma = 10\%$
of typical gap lifts the rate to $0.29$ ($[0.21, 0.37]$). Signal is real
and robust to $\sim10\%$-of-margin noise.

\paragraph{Can we predict which pairs flip?} Not at this sample size. The
quantile heuristic peaks at F1 $= 0.35$ (precision $0.24$, recall $0.64$,
PR-AUC $0.18$); a learned logistic regression on
$|\Delta_1|, b_1, b_2{-}b_1, |g_1|$ does \emph{worse} (PR-AUC $0.13$,
below the baseline $0.16$). Reversal is a population-level phenomenon
here, not per-pair predictable from these features.

\paragraph{Metric choice changes the answer.} AUPR flips $4/45$ tissue
pairs ($0.09$), F1 the same $4/45$, AUROC $0/40$ (Wilson upper $0.09$).
AUROC's scale-invariance preserves cross-tissue rank similarity; AUPR's
base-rate sensitivity does not. \emph{Which metric you pick can swing the
conclusion entirely.}

{\sloppy
\paragraph{Worked example.} A kidney pair (\textsc{scgpt\_attention} vs.\
\textsc{spearman}, TF-source to TF-source-target shift) has gap
$|\Delta_1| = 2.85\times 10^{-2}$ --- safely above any shift family in our
data. An immune pair (\textsc{pearson} vs.\ \textsc{spearman}, all-pairs
to TF-source-target) has gap $8.24\times 10^{-7}$ --- essentially zero,
and indeed one of the 22 reversals, but a coin flip on noise. The
75th-percentile margin cutoff (\S\ref{sec:margin}) excises pairs like the
second, dropping the rate to $0/34$.
\par}

\paragraph{Limitations.} Summary medians only (no replicate noise); one
tissue for the reference axis; one slice for mapping; Prop.~1 is necessary,
not sufficient.

\section{Discussion and Recommendations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three patterns: \emph{(i)}~when candidate-axis reversals happen, the
discrimination term is consistently larger than the base-rate term in
magnitude --- a real data finding, not a tautology;
\emph{(ii)}~candidate-shift reversals disappear once methods are clearly
separated, reference-shift ones do not; \emph{(iii)}~metric choice
alone can swing the conclusion~\cite{demsar2006}. Four practical
recommendations: cluster-bootstrap CIs over method pairs (not Wilson);
state the margin regime; report at least two metrics (AUPR + AUROC) per
rank claim; retain the calibration-vs-base-rate magnitude ratio (not the
sign counts, which are forced by the algebra).
